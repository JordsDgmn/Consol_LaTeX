\subsection{Assessment Reliability and Consistency Analysis}

A critical advantage identified in SimCSE is its deterministic nature, providing identical scores for identical input pairs across multiple iterations. This contrasts with ChatGPT's variable output behavior, which poses significant challenges for educational assessment validity.

\textbf{SimCSE Reliability Characteristics:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reproducibility}: 100\% identical scores for same text pairs
    \item \textbf{Mathematical Foundation}: Cosine similarity in fixed embedding space
    \item \textbf{Consistency}: No variation across time, context, or repeated queries
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Processing}: Preprocessing and embedding procedures yield identical results
\end{itemize}

\textbf{ChatGPT Variability Characteristics:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Non-deterministic Output}: Different scores for identical inputs across sessions
    \item \textbf{Context Dependency}: Influenced by conversation history and prompt variations
    \item \textbf{Subjective Elements}: Incorporates interpretive reasoning that varies over time
    \item \textbf{Length Sensitivity}: Apparent systematic bias toward longer text responses
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Educational Assessment Implications:}

This consistency advantage is crucial for educational assessment validity. Students submitting identical work should receive identical scores regardless of when the assessment occurs or what other submissions have been processed. SimCSE's mathematical approach ensures this fundamental fairness requirement, while ChatGPT's variability introduces assessment uncertainty that undermines educational equity.

The observed length bias in ChatGPT represents a systematic threat to fair assessment, as it may penalize concise but accurate student responses while rewarding verbose but less precise submissions. This bias particularly affects students who demonstrate understanding through efficient, condensed explanations rather than expansive descriptions.

\section{Discussion of Findings}

The comprehensive evaluation results provide substantial evidence for the effectiveness of SimCSE-based educational assessment and the practical viability of the Consol system for real-world educational applications.

\subsection{Implications for Educational Assessment}

\textbf{Objectivity vs Subjectivity in Educational Measurement:}

The research findings highlight a fundamental tension in educational assessment between objective mathematical measurement and subjective interpretive evaluation. SimCSE's mathematical approach offers several advantages:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fairness and Consistency}: Identical content receives identical assessment regardless of external factors
    \item \textbf{Scalability}: Enables consistent evaluation across large student populations without human resource constraints
    \item \textbf{Bias Mitigation}: Eliminates cultural, linguistic, and personal bias that can affect human assessment
    \item \textbf{Immediate Feedback}: Provides instant assessment to support timely learning adjustments
\end{itemize}

However, the research also acknowledges scenarios where subjective assessment provides educational value, particularly in evaluating communication skills, creativity, and critical thinking that extend beyond content recall accuracy.

\textbf{Practical Implementation Considerations:}

The high usability scores (87.5/100 SUS) and strong user acceptance (94\% recommendation likelihood) indicate that SimCSE-based assessment can achieve both technical effectiveness and practical adoption. This combination addresses a critical gap in educational technology where technical sophistication often conflicts with user experience requirements.

\subsection{Contribution to Educational Technology Research}

\textbf{Novel Application of Semantic Similarity:}

This research represents one of the first comprehensive evaluations of SimCSE for educational recall assessment, providing empirical evidence for its effectiveness in academic contexts. The 41.6\% improvement over existing AI assessment methods establishes a new benchmark for educational similarity measurement.

\textbf{Multi-Method Validation Approach:}

The triangulation of SimCSE, ChatGPT, and human teacher assessment provides robust validation that strengthens confidence in the findings. This methodological approach offers a framework for future educational AI evaluation studies.

\textbf{User-Centered Design Integration:}

The combination of technical performance analysis with comprehensive usability evaluation demonstrates how educational technology research can balance algorithmic effectiveness with practical adoption requirements.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Research Directions}

\textbf{Current Study Limitations:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sample Size}: Limited to 5 participants for scoring validation and 14 for usability assessment
    \item \textbf{Domain Specificity}: Focused on biology/science content, requiring validation across other disciplines
    \item \textbf{Temporal Scope}: 3-day assessment period may not capture long-term learning patterns
    \item \textbf{Cultural Context}: Single educational system and language, limiting generalizability
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Future Research Opportunities:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cross-Disciplinary Validation}: Extending evaluation to humanities, social sciences, and technical subjects
    \item \textbf{Longitudinal Studies}: Investigating long-term learning outcomes and retention patterns
    \item \textbf{Multilingual Assessment}: Evaluating SimCSE performance across different languages and cultural contexts
    \item \textbf{Hybrid Assessment Models}: Combining objective similarity measurement with targeted subjective evaluation
\end{itemize}
