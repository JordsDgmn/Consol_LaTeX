# Abstract
The usage of ChatGPT has grown exponentially for various purposes. It’s necessary to assess the motivation to use in academic context. This research aims to assess behavioural intention and dependency on ChatGPT among college students in India. The draft scale consisted of 21 items measuring eight different aspects such as performance anticipation, expected effort, enabling circumstances, educating value, hedonistic intent, habit, intentional behaviour and educatory use. The items were subjected to expert opinion for content validity and were modified accordingly. The study was carried out among college students (N=150) through an online survey. KMO sampling adequacy was found to be 0.919 and Bartlett’s Test was significant (chi-square = 1536.374, df=45, p<0.001) and exploratory factor analysis was conducted to examine the construct validity of the scale. The results of the principal component analysis using varimax rotation indicated that two factors explained 77.71% of the total variance with factor loading values ranging between 0.652 and 0.906. Cronbach coefficient for the total of the 10-item scale under two factors was calculated with Cronbach ⍺ as 0.886. It is found to be reliable and valid to assess behavioural intention and dependency to use ChatGPT in Indian context.

**Keywords:** ChatGPT, behavioural intention, dependency, performance anticipation

# 1. Introduction
ChatGPT has emerged as the most preferred tool recently across various fields. It helps users find information and ideas, translate texts, and provide alternative questions to deepen the understanding of the material (Firaina & Sulisworo, 2023); cultivating writing skills (Punar & Yangın, 2024); significantly increases both utilitarian value and satisfaction in workplace (Jo & Park, 2024).

As adopting AI-powered tools like ChatGPT becomes increasingly prevalent, it is essential to understand the underlying factors influencing users’ behavioral intentions and the potential development of dependency on such technologies, particularly among college students. This demographic segment represents a critical user base, about **33.1% of visitors** (Similar Web, 2023), due to their tech-savvy nature, high digital literacy, and reliance on technology for academic, social, and personal purposes.

Therefore, it is imperative to assess and understand the motive for ChatGPT usage for fine-tuning assignment design as well as customising ChatGPT.

# 2. Theoretical Framework
Behavioural intention is rooted in the **Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA)** and the **Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)**.

TRA (Fishbein & Ajzen, 1975):  
- Intention is the strongest driver of behaviour.  
- Determined by attitudes + subjective norms.

TAM (Davis, 1986, 1989):  
- Attitude  
- Perceived usefulness (PU)  
- Perceived ease of use (PEOU)  
Predicts likelihood of technology adoption.  

TAM has been validated across:  
- e-mail (Adams et al., 1992; Davis, 1989)  
- office software  
- WWW use  
- spreadsheet tools  
- groupware  

Further developments:  
- UTAUT (Ahmad, 2014)  
- UTAUT2 (Ain, Kaur & Waheed, 2015)

Thus, researchers used **UTAUT2 + TRA** to understand ChatGPT behavioural intention.

# Need of the Study
Widespread ChatGPT usage raises academic ethics and misuse concerns (Zhou, 2023).

Guilford’s Structure of Intellect Model (1961):  
Intelligence = abilities combining content, operation, product.

If ChatGPT generates ideas, the student’s cognitive processes may be outsourced — harming critical thinking.

Hence, it is essential to measure motives for ChatGPT use in Indian academia.

# 4. Method

## Item Generation
- 28 items created from UTAUT2 (Ain, Kaur & Waheed, 2016).  
- After expert review, modified to **21 items**, grouped under 8 themes.

## Content Validity
- Sent to 5 experts in psychology + education technology.  
- Items simplified per feedback.

## Rating Scale Format
- 5-point Likert:  
  **Strongly Disagree (1) → Strongly Agree (5)**  
- Negative items reverse scored.  
- Score range: **21 to 105**  
- Higher scores = higher intention to use ChatGPT.

## Data Collection and Sample
- Google Form survey  
- N = 165  
- Age: 17–22  
- Gender: 129 female, 33 male, 3 non-binary  
- Majors: 73.9% Social Sciences, 13.9% Business, 6.7% CS, remainder Medical  

Participation voluntary and anonymous.

## Statistical Analysis
- SPSS  
- Factor analysis  
- Cronbach’s Alpha  
- KMO + Bartlett’s Test

# 6. Conclusion
The study developed a valid and reliable scale to measure **behavioural intention and dependency** on ChatGPT among Indian college students.

- Started with 21 items  
- Factor analysis reduced to **10 items** across **2 factors**:  
  1. **User Attitude**  
  2. **Intentional Behaviour**

Cronbach’s Alpha = **0.886** — highly reliable.

Thus, the tool is valid for measuring ChatGPT behavioural intention and dependency.
