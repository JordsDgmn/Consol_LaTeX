# Abstract
The purpose of note-taking as a tool for assisting memory is vital and therefore the technique adopted important. This paper examines a number of techniques such as the Cornell note-taking method, one that is overwhelming in terms of its popularity and simplicity. A comparative analysis into other methods has been conducted stating the appropriateness to students in an academic learning environment. Research into e-learning and its significance with note-taking has been conducted highlighting the pedagogical principles involved in designing an e-learning environment. Furthermore, the use of Tablet PC’s, annotation applications such as Microsoft OneNote and other factors has been investigated. The influence of multimedia metaphors in particular earcons has been discussed in conjunction with memory and cognitive psychological factors being addressed.

**Key-Words:** Cornell, Earcons, E-Learning, Memory, Multimedia, Microsoft OneNote, Note-Taking, Tablet PC’s.

---

# 1 Introduction
Notes are made to mark information into short paraphrases for the purpose of assisting memory. The core entity associated with note-taking is students who undertake this skill regularly. This paper carries out an investigation into the techniques used to take notes including the most popular Cornell note-taking method. A comparative analysis is performed with the Outlining method and Mapping method stating the strengths and weaknesses of both. Research into e-learning and its relevance to note-taking is discussed. Underlying pedagogical principles have been researched instructing how to create an e-learning environment. Moreover, the use of Tablet PC’s replacing the blackboard and annotation applications such as Microsoft OneNote have been investigated. Following on, the exploration into cognitive psychology studying cognition, memory, attention, concentration and stimuli factors including multimedia influences has been conducted. Finally, the paper is summarised with development ideas.

# 2 Note-Taking Techniques
To capture notes there are many techniques one can adopt however, different techniques are appropriate in different situations [1, 2].

## 2.1 The Cornell Method
The most popular technique to date is the Cornell note-taking method [3].  
It is a systematic approach for arranging and condensing notes without multiple recopying.  
It consists of three main sections:

- **Area A** – Keywords  
- **Area B** – Notes  
- **Area C** – Summary  

**Strengths:**
- Works in almost any subject area  
- Straightforward and effective  
- Helps with organisation and summarisation  
- Encourages reviewing keywords  

At an engineering workshop, students said the method is “simple to do” [4]; it “provides organisation, requires interaction, and concentration,” and reviewing can be done immediately by highlighting keywords.

---

## 2.2 Outlining Method
The Outlining method involves **dashes or indentation** and is not suitable for subject areas like mathematics or physics.

- Facts are indented to the right  
- Relationships represented by indentation  
- Removes need for letters/numbers/roman numerals [5]

**Strengths:**  
- Neatly organized  
- Easy to review  

**Weakness:**  
- Requires full concentration  
- Not suitable when lecture pace is fast  

---

## 2.3 Mapping Method
A graphical representation of lecture content.  
Requires active participation and critical thinking [6].

# 3 E-Learning
E-learning began as a **corporate training solution** delivering academia via the Internet [8].

Technologies involved have grown: internet access, bandwidth, etc. [9]

Two key delivery platforms:

1. **Scheduled Delivery Platforms**  
   - Virtual libraries  
   - Multicasts  
   - Remote labs  
   - Simulate classroom environments [10]  

2. **On-Demand Delivery Platforms**  
   - 24/7 learning support  
   - Fewer time constraints

Pedagogical principles for building e-learning environments (seven parameters) [11]:

1. Institutional support  
2. Course development  
3. Teaching & learning  
4. Course structure  
5. Student support  
6. Faculty support  
7. Evaluation and assessment  

Instructional methodology includes:  
**Analysis → Design → Initial Production → Formative Evaluation → Production**.

Learners classified via learning style inventories:

- Divergers  
- Assimilators  
- Convergers  
- Accommodators [12]

Later modified by strengths/weaknesses [13].

Toward ICT learning styles:

- Activist  
- Reflector  
- Theorist  
- Pragmatist [14]

Student attitudes categorized as:

- Comfort  
- Interactivity  
- Self-satisfaction  
- Value for new technology  
- Experience  
- Content [13]

Theoretical learners tend to have *more negative* attitudes toward ICT [14].

# 3.1 Annotation & E-Learning Tools
In e-learning environments, users can read multimedia books online, annotate, collaborate, and discuss content [16].

Annotation programs:
- Microsoft Word  
- OneNote  
- SharePoint  
- Re:Mark  

**Microsoft OneNote** is most popular due to:
- Insert & edit audio/visual data [17]  
- Record lectures [18]  

**Drawback:** requires every user to have a licensed copy [19].

As learning technologies evolve, *Information Assimilation (IA)* becomes central—gathering and manipulating information, editing, filtering, etc. [20].

---

# 3.2 Person-Centered Teaching
Benefits:
- Electronic module content  
- Course homepage  
- Blackboard discussions  
- Review material for exams [21]

Drawback: expensive [22, 23].

Students supported by facilitators achieve better results and mature individually (experiential learning).

---

# 3.3 Tablet PCs
Tablet PC experiment:
- Connected to data projector  
- Used with Microsoft OneNote to imitate blackboard  
- Benefits: efficiency, convenience, portability, clarity [24]  
- Drawbacks: weak displays, small drawing areas

# 4 Cognition
Lectures are the most common instructional mode. Students **always** take notes regardless of reason [25–30].

Students value note-taking for:
- Encoding  
- Storage  
- Later reviewing

Review + encoding is more effective than encoding alone [33–36].

Students perform better when reviewing **lecturer’s notes**, not their own [37–41].

Effective reviewing methods:
- Summaries  
- Answering questions [2, 42]

---

## Human Memory
Three types:

1. **Sensory memory**  
   - visual/auditory  
   - lasts only an instant [44]

2. **Short-term memory**  
   - up to 30 seconds before forgetting or transferring to LTM [45]

3. **Long-term memory**  
   - cortical storage for extended periods [46]

**Forgetfulness** caused by two interference types [47]:
- Proactive  
- Retroactive  

---

## Multimedia & Cognition
Using multimedia stimuli enhances learning.  
Visual, aural, haptic, and other sensory inputs improve perception [48].

Advantages of computers:
- More flexible than static materials  
- Even auditory metaphors (earcons) increase information communication [50]  

Sound advantages:
- 360-degree perception  
- No need to focus visually [51]

Earcons depend on:
- Rhythm  
- Pitch  
- Intensity  
- Timbre  
- Register [52]

# 5 Conclusion
E-learning technologies can support learning by integrating:
- Lecture notes  
- Handouts  
- Discussions  
- Annotation tools  

The **Cornell Method** is preferred due to:
- Simplicity  
- Universal use across subjects  

Future development involves:
- Computerizing the Cornell system  
- Integrating multimedia metaphors  
- Enhancing usability  
- Personalizing learning experiences
